Buddy

I built Buddy as a small helper that stays on my screen for notes, reminders, and meetings. In meetings it can also tell who is speaking. Everything stays on my own computer.

What Buddy is

Buddy is a small square that sits on top of whatever else I am doing. When I click it, I get a place to write something down, save a task, or turn a meeting into notes I can actually use later. During a meeting it can recognize people by their voice and show who is talking in real time.

I did not want another website login or a paid plan. There is no signup and no subscription. What I write and record stays on my PC.

Who I made it for

  • Anyone who keeps forgetting small todos in the middle of a busy day
  • Anyone who ends a call and cannot remember what was decided or who said it
  • Anyone who wants meeting notes with real names, not just a wall of text
  • Anyone who wants their notes and meetings to stay private on their machine

What it can do

Voice identification in meetings

This is one of the most important parts for me. I enroll a name once by speaking into the mic. In a live meeting Buddy can show who is speaking, and later the transcript can carry those names too. It matches the sound of the voice, not the exact words someone reads.

Meeting notes

I can record a meeting with my mic and the sound from my computer. Buddy then gives me a transcript, a short summary, and action items. With voices enrolled, those notes make more sense because I can see who said what.

Notes and checklists

I can create a note, put tasks under it, and break a big task into smaller ones. I use this for checkpoint lists and daily follow ups.

Reminders

I can set a reminder on a task for later today, tomorrow, or another day. Buddy can also nudge me in the morning about what is still open.

Simply put: Buddy helps me catch thoughts, not lose tasks, and leave meetings knowing who said what, not just what was said. All of that stays on my own machine.

How I use it in a normal day

  1. I start my computer. Buddy can open on its own, so the square is already waiting on my screen.
  2. I click the square. A small window opens with my notes and meeting tools.
  3. I jot a note or task when something comes to mind. I do not need to open a big app or a browser tab for that.
  4. Before meetings that matter, I enroll voices once. Each person speaks for a short time under their name. After that Buddy knows how they sound.
  5. In a meeting, I press Record. Buddy listens to me and to the call audio from my computer. While people talk, it can show who is speaking right now.
  6. When I stop, Buddy prepares a transcript with speaker names where it can, plus a summary and next steps I can turn into tasks.

What Buddy is not

  • It is not a social network or chat app
  • It does not upload my meetings to some company website by default
  • I do not need a pile of tech terms just to use notes and reminders

About privacy

My notes, tasks, and meeting recordings live on my PC. If I set up the optional speech and summary tools, those also run on my machine. The whole point for me is simple: my work stays with me.

Getting started

  1. Install Buddy on a Windows computer using the start instructions in the project.
  2. Find the light brown square on the screen and click it once.
  3. Try New note, add a task, and set a reminder.
  4. On the Meet tab, enroll your voice, then use Test voice to check it.
  5. When you feel ready, try a short meeting recording and watch who is speaking.
If I had to say it in one line: Buddy is like a sticky note that can also take meeting minutes and tell who is talking. It stays private, stays on top, and fits a real workday.
